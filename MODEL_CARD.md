# Model Card: Global Air Pollution AQI Category Classifier

## Model Overview
- **Model Name**: Tuned RandomForestClassifier Pipeline
- **Artifact Path**: `models/air_quality_aqi_category_model.joblib`
- **Metadata Path**: `models/model_metadata.json`
- **Task Type**: Multi-class Classification (6 Air Quality Categories)
- **Framework**: `scikit-learn`, `joblib`

---

## Intended Use & Domain
This model is intended as a demonstration and exploratory classification service to estimate ambient Air Quality Index (AQI) categories from observed pollutant sub-indices (`co_aqi_value`, `ozone_aqi_value`, `no2_aqi_value`, `pm2.5_aqi_value`) and country context.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Advisory Only**: Predictions generated by this model are numerical estimations and **do NOT constitute an official regulatory determination** or formal legal declaration by any statutory environmental agency (e.g. US EPA, CPCB India, EEA).

---

## Target Classes
The model predicts one of 6 discrete standard AQI categories:
1. `Good` (AQI 0–50)
2. `Moderate` (AQI 51–100)
3. `Unhealthy for Sensitive Groups` (AQI 101–150)
4. `Unhealthy` (AQI 151–200)
5. `Very Unhealthy` (AQI 201–300)
6. `Hazardous` (AQI 301+)

---

## Features & Preprocessing Pipeline
1. **Raw Numerical Features**:
   - `co_aqi_value`
   - `ozone_aqi_value`
   - `no2_aqi_value`
   - `pm2.5_aqi_value`
2. **Engineered Numerical Features**:
   - `pollutant_max = max(CO, O3, NO2, PM2.5)`
   - `pollutant_mean = mean(CO, O3, NO2, PM2.5)`
   - `pollutant_sum = sum(CO, O3, NO2, PM2.5)`
   - `pm25_to_ozone_ratio = PM2.5 / (Ozone + 1e-5)`
3. **Categorical Features**:
   - `country_name` (Target/One-Hot Encoded via scikit-learn preprocessing pipeline)

## Limitations, Biases & Leakage Risks (Critical Audit)
1. **Target Derivation & Leakage Risk**:
   In atmospheric science and standard EPA/CPCB protocols, overall `aqi_category` is deterministically derived from `max(pm2.5_aqi, ozone_aqi, no2_aqi, co_aqi)`. Because the features input to the classifier are the sub-indices themselves, the machine learning model essentially learns this deterministic piecewise maximum function rather than underlying meteorological physics.
2. **Class Imbalance**:
   The dataset heavily skews towards `Good` (42.3%) and `Moderate` (39.3%), with `Hazardous` comprising less than 0.9% of samples.
3. **Temporal Dynamics**:
   The baseline dataset does not contain fine-grained temporal timestamps for time-series walk-forward validation; predictions are evaluated cross-sectionally.
